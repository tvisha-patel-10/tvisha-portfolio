<!-- 2. FOUNDING DESIGNER OPERATING MODEL -->
<section id="operating-model" class="cs-section">
  <h4 class="section-label">Founding Designer Role</h4>
  <h2>Operating at startup velocity: owning product, systems, and code</h2>
  <p>
    In an early-stage startup with 20 engineers and operators and only 1 designer, design cannot become a bottleneck. I established a lean operating rhythm designed for speed without sacrificing craft:
  </p>

  <div class="grid-3">
    <div class="grid-card">
      <h3>0→1 Design System</h3>
      <p>
        Built a cohesive Figma library and token architecture (colors, typography, spacing, elevation) from scratch. Standardizing patterns early allowed engineers to build complex interfaces 2x faster without waiting for bespoke mockups for every edge state.
      </p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid-card">
      <h3>Full-Stack Prototyping & Code</h3>
      <p>
        Rather than stopping at static Figma specs, I bridged handoff friction by implementing production-ready HTML, CSS, and TypeScript directly when frontend developers were constrained—shipping UI fixes and polish directly into production.
      </p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid-card">
      <h3>Autonomous Decision-Making</h3>
      <p>
        Partnered directly with founders and data scientists to shape the product roadmap. Made design calls autonomously without waiting for micro-approvals, knowing when to ship rapid MVPs versus when to invest in deep polish.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>

<!-- 3. HOMEPAGE & CONVERSION EXPERIMENT -->
<section id="growth-experiment" class="cs-section">
  <h4 class="section-label">Homepage & Conversion</h4>
  <h2>Pivoting from generic value props to intent-driven conversion (+20% lift)</h2>
  <p>
    Our top-of-funnel acquisition was leaking potential clients. The original homepage suffered from high bounce rates and unclear intent capture.
  </p>

  <div class="grid-2">
    <div class="grid-card">
      <h3>The Problem with V1</h3>
      <p>
        V1 led with abstract relationship-focused headlines (<em>“Unlock Your Relationship with a Personal Coach”</em>) and generic coach headshots. Visitors didn’t immediately understand what specific problems coaching would solve or where to start.
      </p>
      <p style="color: #ef4444; font-weight: 500;">
        Friction: High cognitive overhead before identifying personal need.
      </p>
    </div>
    <div class="grid-card">
      <h3>My Hypothesis for V2</h3>
      <p>
        If we anchor the page on immediate problem diagnosis (<em>“Get unstuck, faster”</em>) with visual category selectors (<strong>My Career</strong>, <strong>My Personal Life</strong>), users can self-select their intent in one click and immediately see concrete outcomes.
      </p>
      <p style="color: #10b981; font-weight: 500;">
        Outcome: Direct clarity, lower barrier to starting assessment.
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>

<!-- 4. CORE WORKFLOWS & AI SYSTEMS -->
<section id="platform-systems" class="cs-section">
  <h4 class="section-label">Core Workflows & AI</h4>
  <h2>Designing dual-sided experiences across clients and coaches</h2>
  <p>
    Managing a dual-sided marketplace means balancing two contrasting user archetypes: clients who demand consumer simplicity, and coaches who need professional data density.
  </p>

  <h3>1. Building Trust in AI During Client Onboarding</h3>
  <p>
    Strawberry leverages AI models to synthesize client preferences and match them with the right coach. However, coaching is deeply personal—clients feared their sensitive vulnerabilities would be harvested. I designed transparent onboarding and consent checkpoints explaining our de-identification, aggregation, and data privacy models upfront, boosting onboarding completion.
  </p>
  <div class="full-width-media">
    <img
      src="/images/strawberry/image.png"

  <h3>2. Coach Workstation & Smart Reminders</h3>
  <p>
    For coaches managing dozens of active clients, I redesigned the Client Details Command Center: consolidating timelines, action items, session history, and automated reminder cues into a single view.
  </p>
